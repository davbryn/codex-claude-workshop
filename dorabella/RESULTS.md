# Dorabella results

All numbers are for the consensus transcription unless marked. Scores are mean quadgram
log10-probability per letter-quadruple: 87 letters of real text score about −4.0 to −4.2
under their own language's model, and the same letters shuffled about −6.5.

Two rules apply to every row. **Decoys:** the same search is run on shuffled copies of
the cipher (same glyphs, random order, so no message), and the cipher must beat them.
**Positive control:** held-out text enciphered the family's way must be cracked by the
same search, or the family is "untestable" rather than "ruled out".

**The vowel-less near-miss.** A verifier re-implemented the test independently. It
found that the claimed p came from one lucky seed against under-converged decoys. At a
converged budget the p-value is 0.010–0.013, and the family-wise p over the 24 model ×
direction tests is 0.035–0.055. Shuffles that keep the cipher's own glyph-pair counts
reproduce 60% of the excess, and the plaintext does not segment into words.

**The phoneme point prediction.** The Cairn analysis predicted that a phoneme cipher
solved with the letter model would land at Dorabella's own letter score. It does, at
−4.79 ± 0.16. But word-reversed, vowel-less, Latin, French, German and Italian texts
land at the same place, so this does not discriminate. Direct phoneme decoding gives no
signal.

## 4. The direction motif

Run `python -m dorabella.structure`. Read only the direction each glyph faces, and the
pattern SE NW SE N S SE occurs at positions 46, 59 and 70, with different arc counts
every time. Every reader agrees on all 18 directions.

| statistic | Dorabella | shuffles | p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| repeated adjacent pairs, whole glyph | 23 | 12.5 ± 3.4 | 0.006 |
| repeated adjacent pairs, direction only | 125 | 82.8 ± 8.0 | 0.0002 |
| repeated adjacent pairs, arc count only | 392 | 397.7 ± 13.5 | 0.60 |
| mirror pairs, same arc count | 13 | 5.1 ± 2.2 | 0.003 |
| longest direction pattern written 3 times | 6 | 2.7 ± 0.5 | 1e-5 |

- **It is all of the direction structure.** Mask the three copies and the
  direction-pair excess vanishes (z +0.86). Given the direction sequence and the
  arc-by-direction table, 13 same-arc mirror pairs is exactly what is expected
  (11.5 ± 2.4). So the mirror-pair excess is a direction effect.
- **It is unusual for English under a natural key.** Across 120,000 English windows
  under random, Elgar-1924, keyword-mixed and mirror-optimised keys, three copies of a
  direction 6-gram with pairwise different arcs occurs about once in 3,000–4,000. An
  adversarial key built to make directions collide reaches only 1 in 280. Correcting for
  the statistic having been chosen after seeing it gives p ≈ 0.001–0.005 (0.005–0.009 in
  a more repetitive register). Free arcs would make it about 30–40× likelier.
- **If the copies are the same plaintext, no simple arc rule fits.** Ruled out:
  - every periodic arc key whose period links the copies (≤ 60);
  - ciphertext autokey on the previous 1–3 glyphs, look-ahead of 1–3, plaintext autokey
    with memory 1–5;
  - per-direction homophone cycling, arcs by line, arcs by neighbouring directions;
  - all progressive Vigenère-style rules.

  What remains: the arcs are free or a second channel, the key is long or running, or
  this is a coincidence.
- **The homophone reading fails on frequency.** If SE1, SE2 and SE3 were one letter, it
  would be 26% of the text. That happens in about 5 of every 100,000 English windows,
  and at most 1–3% in any European language tested.

## 6. Elgar's notebook page

Decoded independently with the plain key and confirmed against published readings:
MARCO ELGAR, A VERY OLD CYPHER (round and square glyphs), DO YOU GO TO LONDON. Across
all 3,072 fixed disc settings, only the plain key makes these lines words; 0 of 300
shuffles of line B read as words at any setting. The disc drawings encode no rotations,
and the settings they suggest give nothing on these lines or on Dorabella. The page
dates from 1924 or later.
